# Datasets for static-camera household object-interaction detection — survey and recommendations

Scope: pick-up / put-down / open / close / carry from a fixed wall or counter camera, with temporal boundaries, per-object identity, outcome verification, and abstention on quiet intervals; commercial deployment intended. All facts below come from primary pages, papers, or license files fetched in this session unless marked **(paper, not re-verified)** or **(inferred)**. Three official pages were unreachable (50 Salads, Breakfast, CAD-120) and are marked as such. The session's web-search quota ran out midway, so the last third of the work used direct fetches only.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **No existing dataset matches the target setting** (consumer wall/counter camera, many real homes, dense pick/put/open/close labels, commercial license). Every close match on content is non-commercial; every commercially clean set is either egocentric, robot-embodied, or a lab/tripod capture.
2. **Ego-Exo4D is the strongest commercially usable source of real humans manipulating kitchen objects seen from static cameras**: 4 tripod GoPros per take, ~650 cooking takes, timestamped atomic descriptions (~28/min), keystep intervals covering ~89% of cooking time, and 1 fps exocentric object masks with persistent track IDs. Its license (Ego4D family) explicitly allows training models for "commercial or noncommercial product development" while forbidding the data itself from appearing in any product.
3. **Two license conflicts must be resolved by email before relying on them**:
   - **HD-EPIC**: project site says CC BY-NC 4.0 (commercial contact uob-epic-kitchens@bristol.ac.uk); the data.bris repository record for the same DOI says CC BY 4.0; the annotations repo has no license file. Treat as non-commercial until Bristol confirms otherwise.
   - **EPFL-Smart-Kitchen-30**: both Zenodo records carry the machine-readable license `cc-by-4.0`, but the record descriptions say "released under the non-commercial CC BY-NC 4.0 license". Treat as non-commercial until the authors confirm.
4. **Toyota Smarthome Untrimmed (TSU) is the best held-out evaluation set on content** (untrimmed, densely labeled, 7 fixed cameras in a real apartment, ~190 h) but its license bans any commercial use including "train machine learning models for commercial applications"; Toyota offers commercial licensing by email.
5. **Something-Something v2 is unusable commercially**: Qualcomm's license defines "Result" as any model derived from the data and restricts everything to "Research Use ... solely for non-profit research purposes".
6. **Synthetic route**: several commercially clean generators exist for kitchens, articulated fixtures, and object-ID/box/mask ground truth (Infinigen Indoors BSD-3, AI2-THOR/ProcTHOR Apache-2.0, Isaac Sim Apache-2.0 + NVIDIA materials terms, Kubric Apache-2.0, RoboCasa MIT + CC BY 4.0 assets, ReplicaCAD CC BY 4.0), but **none renders realistic human hands manipulating objects out of the box**, and the obvious human-asset sources are non-commercial (Unity SyntheticHumans, BEDLAM/SMPL-X, BEHAVIOR-1K assets, HSSD).

## 3. License traps worth knowing

- **Toyota Smarthome / TSU**: the ban explicitly names "train machine learning models for commercial applications"; even evaluating a commercial product is a "use for commercial purposes" under the "Any other use" clause. Get written permission (email in the license) before touching it in a commercial pipeline.
- **Ego4D / Ego-Exo4D**: the grant is unusually favorable (commercial product development with trained models is allowed), but the data may not "appear in or be visible in any program, dataset, or product" and cannot be redistributed; keep frames out of demos, docs, and shipped test suites. Ego-Exo4D requires a separate signature even if Ego4D was signed.
- **Something-Something v2**: any model trained on it is a "Result" restricted to non-profit research; Qualcomm reserves injunctions and disgorgement for commercial use.
- **RH20T**: split license by scene ID; scenes 6–10 forbid commercial use of models trained on them.
- **CC BY-SA (OakInk2, TACO, RH20T-C, HOT3D sequences)**: ShareAlike is settled for derived datasets and unsettled for trained models; treat derived label files as SA and get counsel on model weights.
- **HD-EPIC and EPFL-Smart-Kitchen-30**: conflicting statements between repository metadata and author pages; assume the more restrictive statement until resolved in writing.
- **Charades / Action Genome / Something-Else**: MIT-licensed annotation files do not override the non-commercial video license underneath.

For the human-appearance half of the problem there is no clean turnkey path: Unity SyntheticHumans, BEDLAM, HSSD, and BEHAVIOR-1K assets are all non-commercial, and the SMPL-X body model that underlies most avatar pipelines is non-commercial by default. A practical compromise is to train object-motion and articulation-state heads on synthetic renders (domain-randomized) and train the hand/contact heads on the commercially usable real datasets in Section 5; if realistic synthetic humans are required, budget for a commercially licensed character pipeline and verify the license of every human asset separately.

## 5. Ranked recommendations

### Pretraining an interaction classifier (3)

**1. Ego-Exo4D, cooking subset, exocentric views** — https://ego-exo4d-data.org/ · docs: https://docs.ego-exo4d-data.org/getting-started/
License conclusion: **conditional YES**. The docs state the agreement "covers the use of the dataset for research purposes and commercial use"; the Ego4D-family text permits training models for "commercial or noncommercial product development" but forbids redistributing data or letting it "appear in ... any product". Sign at ego4d.dev and archive the executed text.
Why: the only large corpus of real people manipulating real kitchen objects seen from stationary cameras (4 tripod GoPros per take; cooking 678 takes / 564 h combined). Derive your five classes from the 189K timestamped cooking atomic descriptions (verb parsing, using the `best_exo` flag to pick the view), use keystep intervals (~89% coverage) to bound positives, and use the 1 fps exocentric object masks with persistent `track_id` (888K cooking exo masks) to supervise per-object identity and "object left the counter" outcomes. Caveat: tripods at body height rather than counter-mounted; skilled cooks; ~14 TiB recommended download.

**2. Ego4D Hands & Objects (state-change clips), with HoloAssist as a clean supplement** — https://ego4d-data.org/docs/benchmarks/hands-and-objects/ · https://holoassist.github.io/
License conclusion: Ego4D **conditional YES** (same terms as above); HoloAssist **YES** (CDLA-Permissive-2.0, no restrictions on Results).
Why: PRE/PNR/POST frames with object-of-change boxes and ~21K balanced negative clips are the closest existing supervision for outcome verification and abstention; HoloAssist adds 169 h of permissively licensed fine-grained hand-object action intervals. Both are egocentric, so use them for the temporal-boundary and state-change heads only, with viewpoint augmentation, not for the wall-camera appearance model.

**3. RH20T-C human-demonstration videos (scenes 0001–0005), supplemented by DROID and OakInk2** — https://rh20t.github.io/ · https://droid-dataset.github.io/ · https://oakink.net/v2/
License conclusion: RH20T-C **YES (CC BY-SA 4.0)**; DROID **YES (CC BY 4.0)**; OakInk2 **YES (CC BY-SA 4.0)**. Do not touch RH20T scenes 0006–0010 (CC BY-NC 4.0, models included).
Why: RH20T-C provides real humans performing 147 manipulation tasks in front of 8–10 fixed RGB-D cameras — the closest commercially clean analogue of a counter camera watching a hand pick up and put down objects — while DROID contributes 564 real household scenes with two fixed stereo cameras and language task labels for scene/camera-placement robustness, and OakInk2 contributes object 6-DoF ground truth (kitchen-table scenario) for the "lifted clear of the surface" outcome head. Caveats: lab tabletops, task-level rather than frame-level labels in RH20T/DROID, robot embodiment in DROID, ShareAlike obligations for derived label files.

### Held-out evaluation on a static home camera (2)

**1. Toyota Smarthome Untrimmed (TSU)** — https://project.inria.fr/toyotasmarthome/ · license: https://project.inria.fr/toyotasmarthome/files/2020/12/License_v2.pdf
License conclusion: **NO under the standard license; commercial license available by email** (gianpiero.francesca@toyota-europe.com). Do not use it to evaluate a commercial system until permission is in writing.
Why: the only untrimmed, densely labeled, multi-camera dataset recorded by fixed cameras in a real apartment (536 videos × ~21 min ≈ 190 h, 51 concurrent classes including object interactions, 18 senior residents, faces blurred). Dense labels make false-events-per-hour measurable; the class set is ADL-level, so map your primitives to its object-interaction classes and treat the rest as abstention intervals.

**2. EPFL-Smart-Kitchen-30, exocentric views** (fallback: HOMAGE) — https://zenodo.org/records/15535461 · https://zenodo.org/records/15551913 · fallback https://homeactiongenome.org/
License conclusion: **CONFLICT — treat as NO until the authors confirm** (Zenodo license field cc-by-4.0 vs description "non-commercial CC BY-NC 4.0"). HOMAGE fallback is **NO** (non-commercial competition terms).
Why: nine fixed RGB-D views including counter-, stove-, and sink-level cameras, with 60,189 fine-grained verb-noun segments at 33.78 per minute over complete recipe-to-cleanup sessions — the densest static-camera kitchen labels available and a direct test of temporal boundaries and abstention. If EPFL confirms CC BY 4.0 it becomes the only commercially clean dense static-kitchen benchmark; if not, HOMAGE (2 real houses, 453 atomic actions with boundaries, scene graphs) is the most home-realistic non-commercial alternative.

Practical note: none of the above covers many consumer homes with wall-mounted cameras; a small self-recorded evaluation set with dense labels remains necessary for false-events-per-hour claims. HD-EPIC's object-movement schema (start/end, pickup location, placement location, fixture ID, plus open/close from narrations) is the label design to copy for that set, even though its data should be treated as non-commercial.
